The fraction constellation

Small parts.
Whole new discoveries.

A fraction tells us how much of a whole we have. Build one, change its pieces, and discover how different fractions can describe the same amount.

Build a fraction ↗ Try a challenge

01 / Meet the parts

Two numbers. One relationship.

The numerator

The top number counts the parts we are considering. In 3/4, the numerator is 3.

The denominator

The bottom number tells us how many equal parts make one whole. In 3/4, the denominator is 4.

Equal parts matter

Fourths must be equal portions of the same whole. Three fourths means three of those four equal parts.

03 / Make a connection

Different pieces. Same amount.

This model follows the fraction you built above. Multiply its numerator and denominator by the same positive whole number to make an equivalent fraction.

Original: 0/4

Equivalent: 0/8

0/4 = 0/8. The pieces get smaller, but the selected amount stays the same.

Why does it work? We divide the same whole into more pieces. Both bars represent an equally sized whole.

05 / Explore relationships

Five ways to explore fractions.

These models use equally sized wholes. Explore each activity separately and ask for an explanation whenever you need one.

Compare Add Subtract Multiply Divide

≶

Compare fractions

Which amount is greater? Compare the highlighted portions of two equally sized wholes.

A larger denominator means smaller individual pieces, not automatically a larger fraction.

+

Add fractions

Combine portions with matching denominators. These challenges keep the total at one whole or less.

Add the numerators. Keep the denominator because the size of each piece stays the same.

−

Subtract fractions

Take one portion away from another. These challenges use matching denominators and answers of zero or more.

Subtract the numerators. Keep the denominator because you are still counting the same-sized pieces.

×

Multiply fractions

Find a fraction of another fraction. The model divides one whole into rows and columns. The golden overlap shows the product.

Multiply the numerators together. Multiply the denominators together. For example, 1/2 of 1/3 is 1/6.

÷

Divide fractions

Discover how many portions of one size fit inside another amount. These challenges begin with matching denominators.

For example, 3/4 ÷ 1/4 asks how many quarter-sized portions fit inside three quarters. The answer is 3. Some challenges include part of a portion.
